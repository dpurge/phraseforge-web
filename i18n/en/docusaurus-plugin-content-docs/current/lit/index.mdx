# Lithuanian language

## Genetic and structural classification

Indo-European family, Baltic branch, Eastern Baltic subgroup. One of the most archaic living Indo-European languages — preserving features close to Sanskrit and Proto-Indo-European (seven cases, mobile accent).

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 3 million speakers. Lithuania, with minorities in Poland (Suwałki region, Puńsk), Belarus, Latvia, Russia (Kaliningrad Oblast), and the USA.

## Dialects

Two groups: *Aukštaitian* (High Lithuanian — the basis of the literary standard, divided into Western, Southern and Eastern) and *Samogitian* (*Žemaitiškas*) — strongly distinct, sometimes treated as a separate language.

## Phonetics

Long and short vowels, diphthongs. Mobile accent with three pitch contours (circumflex, acute, short). Hard and palatalized consonants.

## Writing system

Latin alphabet with diacritics (*ą ę į ų — with ogonek; č š ž — with háček; ė, y, ū*). 32 letters in total.

## History

Separated from Proto-Baltic around the 5th-6th century AD. The first book in Lithuanian was Martynas Mažvydas's catechism (1547). The modern literary standard was established in the 19th-20th centuries (Jonas Jablonskis).
