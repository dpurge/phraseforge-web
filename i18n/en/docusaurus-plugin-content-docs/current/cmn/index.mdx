# Chinese language

## Genetic and structural classification

Sino-Tibetan family, Sinitic branch. This page describes Mandarin (普通话, *Pǔtōnghuà*) — the standard language based on the Beijing dialect. An isolating and tonal language.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 920 million native Mandarin speakers, around 1.1 billion in total. Mainland China, Taiwan, Singapore, and the diaspora.

## Dialects

A network of Sinitic languages — often called dialects — that are mutually unintelligible: Mandarin (官话), Wu (吴, including Shanghainese), Cantonese (粤), Min (闽), Hakka, Gan, Xiang. Within Mandarin: Northern, Southwestern, Lower Yangtze.

## Phonetics

Four tones plus a neutral tone. Syllables are built from an initial (consonant) and a final (vowel plus optional -n / -ng). No consonant clusters.

## Writing system

Logographic writing — *hànzì* characters (汉字). Two variants: Simplified (mainland China, Singapore) and Traditional (Taiwan, Hong Kong). Romanization: *pinyin* (普通话) and *zhùyīn* (Taiwan).

## History

Classical Chinese (*wényán*, 文言) was used as the written language until the 20th century. After the May Fourth Movement of 1919 — transition to vernacular (*báihuà*, 白话). *Pǔtōnghuà* was standardized after 1955.
