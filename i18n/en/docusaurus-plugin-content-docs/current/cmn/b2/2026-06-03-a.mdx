# 在太空怀孕和生育人类：待解的科学问题

航天领域正在实现许多重要目标。

今年五月，美国太空总署（NASA）公布了建造月球基地的计划，这个基地将由核能和太阳能供电，耗资200亿美元。

NASA希望到2032年，人类可以在月球上长期居住。

NASA也计划首次将人类送上火星，这次旅行大约需要七到十个月。

如果人类想长期在地球以外的地方生活，就需要在那里生育后代，这意味着我们要适应新的环境。

地球有磁场和厚厚的大气层保护，但月球和火星等地方的表面会受到强烈的太空辐射。另外，因为它们的体积比地球小，所以重力也比地球弱很多。

去这些星球的过程也要考虑——长时间的太空飞行可能影响人体和生育能力，我们对此还不完全了解。

虽然在其他星球或太空中怀孕听起来像科幻故事，但现在越来越多的科学家正在深入研究这个问题。

妮可·麦克弗森博士是澳大利亚阿德莱德大学的讲师，也是一个研究精子和胚胎生物学的团队负责人。

她和丈夫看了太空纪录片后，开始研究在太空中生育的可能性。

麦克弗森说：“这开始只是一个有趣的科幻小想法，但现在成了一个重要的研究领域。”

她和团队在一个模拟生殖道的环境中，测试了精子在微重力下（像太空中的重力环境）的运动能力。

他们发现，有些精子会迷失方向，到达终点的精子数量比在正常重力下的少了一半。

虽然这项研究没有模拟火星等星球的重力，但麦克弗森认为，它显示了地球重力对精子正常活动的重要性。

她说：“这说明精子在自然受孕过程中需要地球的引力。”

重力带来的问题不只影响受孕。研究人员也发现微重力会影响小鼠胚胎最初24小时的发育。

麦克弗森说：“我们看到了不好的结果——胚胎发育不正常，早期胚胎也有些缺陷。”

美国太空总署的高级科学家法蒂·卡鲁亚博士说：“微重力会影响细胞的分裂、组织和交流，这些对受精和早期胚胎发育都很重要。”

太空探索的第二个大挑战是辐射。

我们知道辐射对生殖细胞有害，生殖细胞是形成男性精子和女性卵子的特殊细胞。

地球磁场保护我们不受太空中大部分强烈辐射的伤害，但在地球磁场之外，比如在火星上，情况就完全不同了。

土耳其健康科学大学的航空航天医学专家阿卜杜勒·拉赫曼·恩金博士说，银河宇宙射线（GCR）是超新星爆发等宇宙事件产生的。

这些射线可以穿透飞船的保护层，进入人体组织，其强度比地球上的辐射强很多。

他研究了一个问题：未来去火星的任务中，如果宇航员怀孕，她们受到的银河宇宙射线辐射量是否会超过地球上认为对怀孕安全的水平。

他说：“不幸的是，答案似乎是肯定的。”

从地球到火星大约六个月的旅程中，怀孕的宇航员可能会受到90到300毫西弗的辐射。

这比美国国家辐射防护和测量委员会建议的怀孕期间职业暴露限值5毫西弗高很多。

这么高的辐射量可能会给早期发育的胚胎和胎儿带来很大风险，可能损害他们的DNA。

恩金说：“早期发育时细胞分裂很快，器官也在形成，所以即使DNA结构、修复或细胞生长有很小的破坏，也可能导致终身的健康问题。”

这些风险包括流产、生长发育迟缓、先天性畸形、神经系统问题，以及儿童时期患癌症的风险增加。

恩金博士表示，有些方法，比如飞船的防护罩或在太阳活动较弱时安排任务，可以降低风险，但要完全阻止宇宙射线“非常困难”。

卡鲁亚指出，辐射和微重力一起“从根本上影响生物学”，但还有其他问题。

他说：“太空飞行还会打乱荷尔蒙平衡、睡眠周期和昼夜节律，这些都和生殖健康紧密相关。”

他解释说，当这些因素共同作用时，会影响精子或卵子的质量，以及怀孕期间胎儿生长需要的荷尔蒙等方面。

了解人类是否能在太空中生育，对于思考如何在地球之外延续生命非常重要。

瑞典乌普萨拉大学分子与细胞生物学教授扬-贝恩德·斯图肯博格说，这不仅关系到人类在太空生育的问题，也关系到宇航员的健康安全。

他指出，即使人类现在还没有在太空生育，宇航员和他们的生殖细胞已经暴露在太空环境中。研究辐射对生殖细胞的影响，也许能帮助我们找到保护方法。

斯图肯博格说：“如果皮肤细胞（因为太空旅行）出现问题，那么当人去世时，这个皮肤细胞也会随之消失。”

但即使宇航员现在没有在太空生育，生殖细胞的损伤也可能遗传给下一代。

他说：“所以，我认为这些研究非常重要。”

麦克弗森表示，这个领域的更多研究能帮助我们了解人类生物学中更基本的问题。

例如，她的研究发现，在实验中能成功到达终点的精子，似乎有一些特点，这些特点也让它们在受精和早期发育时更有优势。

她说：“如果我们能更深入地了解这些精子为何能成功，那么我们可以在进行体外受精（IVF）或动物体外受精时做出更好的选择。”

专家们也认为，随着越来越多女性进入太空，关于太空生育的研究需要扩大，避免只关注男性。

斯图肯博格提到的研究显示，女性宇航员在长期任务中主要通过服用避孕药来阻止月经。

但麦克弗森说，太空环境对月经周期的影响还需要更多研究。

她说：“我们目前还不能真正评估这个问题。”

卡鲁亚表示，有证据表明卵巢功能可能受辐射影响，微重力也可能影响卵子成熟。

他说，现在最大的知识空缺之一是怀孕过程本身。

他说：“我们几乎没有关于哺乳动物在太空中如何完整怀孕的现代数据，特别是在受精卵着床、胎盘发育和器官形成方面。”

几乎所有人都同意，还有很多重要工作要做。

斯图肯博格说：“这不应该是一个禁忌话题……我们现在就必须考虑这个问题。”

# Zài Tàikōng Huáiyùn hé Shēngyù Rénlèi: Dài Jiě de Kēxué Wèntí

Hángtiān lǐngyù zhèngzài shíxiàn xǔduō zhòngyào mùbiāo.

Jīnnián wǔyuè, Měiguó Tàikōng Zǒngshǔ (NASA) gōngbùle jiànzào yuèqiú jīdì de jìhuà, zhège jīdì jiāng yóu hénéng hé tàiyángnéng gōngdiàn, hàozī 200 yì Měiyuán.

NASA xīwàng dào 2032 nián, rénlèi kěyǐ zài yuèqiú shàng chángqī jūzhù.

NASA yě jìhuà shǒucì jiāng rénlèi sòng shàng Huǒxīng, zhè cì lǚxíng dàyuē xūyào qī dào shí gè yuè.

Rúguǒ rénlèi xiǎng chángqī zài dìqiú yǐwài de dìfang shēnghuó, jiù xūyào zài nàlǐ shēngyù hòudài, zhè yìwèizhe wǒmen yào shìyìng xīn de huánjìng.

Dìqiú yǒu cíhǎichǎng hé hòuhòu de dàqìcéng bǎohù, dàn yuèqiú hé Huǒxīng děng dìfang de biǎomiàn huì shòudào qiángliè de tàikōng fúshè. Lìngwài, yīnwèi tāmen de tǐjī bǐ dìqiú xiǎo, suǒyǐ zhònglì yě bǐ dìqiú ruò hěn duō.

Qù zhèxiē xīngqiú de guòchéng yě yào kǎolǜ — cháng shíjiān de tàikōng fēixíng kěnéng yǐngxiǎng réntǐ hé shēngyù nénglì, wǒmen duì cǐ hái bù wánquán liǎojiě.

Suīrán zài qítā xīngqiú huò tàikōng zhōng huáiyùn tīng qǐlái xiàng kēhuàn gùshì, dàn xiànzài yuè lái yuè duō de kēxuéjiā zhèngzài shēnrù yánjiū zhège wèntí.

Nīkē Mài Kèfú Sēn bóshì shì Àodàlìyà Ādéláidé Dàxué de jiǎngshī, yě shì yí gè yánjiū jīngzǐ hé pēitāi shēngwùxué de tuánduì fùzérén.

Tā hé zhàngfu kànle tàikōng jìlùpiàn hòu, kāishǐ yánjiū zài tàikōng zhōng shēngyù de kěnéngxìng.

Mài Kèfú Sēn shuō: "Zhè kāishǐ zhǐ shì yí gè yǒuqù de kēhuàn xiǎo xiǎngfǎ, dàn xiànzài chéngle yí gè zhòngyào de yánjiū lǐngyù."

Tā hé tuánduì zài yí gè mónǐ shēngzhídào de huánjìng zhōng, cèshìle jīngzǐ zài wēizhònglì xià (xiàng tàikōng zhōng de zhònglì huánjìng) de yùndòng nénglì.

Tāmen fāxiàn, yǒuxiē jīngzǐ huì míshī fāngxiàng, dàodá zhōngdiǎn de jīngzǐ shùliàng bǐ zài zhèngcháng zhònglì xià de shǎole yí bàn.

Suīrán zhè xiàng yánjiū méiyǒu mónǐ Huǒxīng děng xīngqiú de zhònglì, dàn Mài Kèfú Sēn rènwéi, tā xiǎnshìle dìqiú zhònglì duì jīngzǐ zhèngcháng huódòng de zhòngyàoxìng.

Tā shuō: "Zhè shuōmíng jīngzǐ zài zìrán shòuyùn guòchéng zhōng xūyào dìqiú de yǐnlì."

Zhònglì dài lái de wèntí bùzhǐ yǐngxiǎng shòuyùn. Yánjiū rényuán yě fāxiàn wēizhònglì huì yǐngxiǎng xiǎoshǔ pēitāi zuìchū 24 xiǎoshí de fāyù.

Mài Kèfú Sēn shuō: "Wǒmen kàn dàole bù hǎo de jiéguǒ — pēitāi fāyù bù zhèngcháng, zǎoqī pēitāi yě yǒuxiē quēxiàn."

Měiguó Tàikōng Zǒngshǔ de gāojí kēxuéjiā Fǎdì Kǎlǔyà bóshì shuō: "Wēizhònglì huì yǐngxiǎng xìbāo de fēnliè, zǔzhī hé jiāoliú, zhèxiē duì shòujīng hé zǎoqī pēitāi fāyù dōu hěn zhòngyào."

Tàikōng tànsuǒ de dì èr ge dà tiǎozhàn shì fúshè.

Wǒmen zhīdào fúshè duì shēngzhí xìbāo yǒuhài, shēngzhí xìbāo shì xíngchéng nánxìng jīngzǐ hé nǚxìng luǎnzǐ de tèshū xìbāo.

Dìqiú cíhǎichǎng bǎohù wǒmen bù shòu tàikōng zhōng dà bùfen qiángliè fúshè de shānghài, dàn zài dìqiú cíhǎichǎng zhīwài, bǐrú zài Huǒxīng shàng, qíngkuàng jiù wánquán bùtóng le.

Tǔěrqí Jiànkāng Kēxué Dàxué de hángkōng hángtiān yīxué zhuānjiā Ābùdùlè Lāhèmán Ēnjīn bóshì shuō, yínhé yǔzhòu shèxiàn (GCR) shì chāoxīn xīng bàofā děng yǔzhòu shìjiàn chǎnshēng de.

Zhèxiē shèxiàn kěyǐ chuāntòu fēichuán de bǎohùcéng, jìnrù réntǐ zǔzhī, qí qiángdù bǐ dìqiú shàng de fúshè qiáng hěn duō.

Tā yánjiūle yí gè wèntí: Wèilái qù Huǒxīng de rènwu zhōng, rúguǒ yǔhángyuán huáiyùn, tāmen shòudào de yínhé yǔzhòu shèxiàn fúshèliàng shìfǒu huì chāoguò dìqiú shàng rènwéi duì huáiyùn ānquán de shuǐpíng.

Tā shuō: "Bùxìng de shì, dá'àn sìhū shì kěndìng de."

Cóng dìqiú dào Huǒxīng dàyuē liù gè yuè de lǚchéng zhōng, huáiyùn de yǔhángyuán kěnéng huì shòudào 90 dào 300 háoxīfú de fúshè.

Zhè bǐ Měiguó Guójiā Fúshè Fánghù hé Cèliáng Wěiyuánhuì jiànyì de huáiyùn qíjiān zhíyè bàolù xiànzhí 5 háoxīfú gāo hěn duō.

Zhème gāo de fúshèliàng kěnéng huì gěi zǎoqī fāyù de pēitāi hé tāi'ér dài lái hěn dà fēngxiǎn, kěnéng sǔnhài tāmen de DNA.

Ēnjīn shuō: "Zǎoqī fāyù shí xìbāo fēnliè hěn kuài, qìguān yě zài xíngchéng, suǒyǐ jíshǐ DNA jiégòu, xiūfù huò xìbāo shēngzhǎng yǒu hěn xiǎo de pòhuài, yě kěnéng dǎozhì zhōngshēn de jiànkāng wèntí."

Zhèxiē fēngxiǎn bāokuò liúchǎn, shēngzhǎng fāyù chíhuǎn, xiāntiān xìng jīxíng, shénjīng xìtǒng wèntí, yǐjí értóng shíqī huàn gān'āi de fēngxiǎn zēngjiā.

Ēnjīn bóshì biǎoshì, yǒuxiē fāngfǎ, bǐrú fēichuán de fánghùzhào huò zài tàiyáng huódòng jiào ruò shí ānpái rènwu, kěyǐ jiàngdī fēngxiǎn, dàn yào wánquán zǔzhǐ yǔzhòu shèxiàn "fēicháng kùnnan".

Kǎlǔyà zhǐchū, fúshè hé wēizhònglì yīqǐ "cóng gēnběn shàng yǐngxiǎng shēngwùxué", dàn hái yǒu qítā wèntí.

Tā shuō: "Tàikōng fēixíng hái huì dǎluàn hè'ěrméng pínghéng, shuìmián zhōuqī hé zhòuyè jiélǜ, zhèxiē dōu hé shēngzhí jiànkāng jǐnmì xiāngguān."

Tā jiěshì shuō, dāng zhèxiē yīnsù gòngtóng zuòyòng shí, huì yǐngxiǎng jīngzǐ huò luǎnzǐ de zhìliàng, yǐjí huáiyùn qíjiān tāi'ér shēngzhǎng xūyào de hè'ěrméng děng fāngmiàn.

Liǎojiě rénlèi shìfǒu néng zài tàikōng zhōng shēngyù, duìyú sīkǎo rúhé zài dìqiú zhīwài yánxù shēngmìng fēicháng zhòngyào.

Ruìdiǎn Wūpǔsàlā Dàxué fēnzǐ yǔ xìbāo shēngwùxué jiàoshòu Yáng-Bèi'ēndé Sītúkēnbógé shuō, zhè bùjǐn guānxì dào rénlèi zài tàikōng shēngyù de wèntí, yě guānxì dào yǔhángyuán de jiànkāng ānquán.

Tā zhǐchū, jíshǐ rénlèi xiànzài hái méiyǒu zài tàikōng shēngyù, yǔhángyuán hé tāmen de shēngzhí xìbāo yǐjīng bàolù zài tàikōng huánjìng zhōng. Yánjiū fúshè duì shēngzhí xìbāo de yǐngxiǎng, yěxǔ néng bāngzhù wǒmen zhǎodào bǎohù fāngfǎ.

Sītúkēnbógé shuō: "Rúguǒ pífū xìbāo (yīnwèi tàikōng lǚxíng) chūxiàn wèntí, nàme dāng rén qùshì shí, zhège pífū xìbāo yě huì suí zhī xiāoshī."

# Pregnancy and human reproduction in space: unresolved scientific questions

The field of spaceflight is achieving many important goals.

This May, the United States space agency NASA announced plans to build a base on the Moon, powered by nuclear and solar energy, at a cost of 20 billion US dollars.

NASA hopes that by 2032 humans will be able to live on the Moon permanently.

NASA also plans to send humans to Mars for the first time, a journey that will take about seven to ten months.

If humans want to live long-term beyond Earth, they will have to reproduce there, which means that we have to adapt to a new environment.

Earth is protected by a magnetic field and a thick atmosphere, but the surfaces of places like the Moon and Mars are exposed to intense cosmic radiation. In addition, because they are smaller than Earth, gravity there is much weaker.

The journey to these planets must also be taken into account — long-duration spaceflight may affect the human body and fertility, and we do not yet fully understand these effects.

Although becoming pregnant on other planets or in space sounds like science fiction, more and more scientists are now investigating this question in depth.

Dr. Nicole McPherson is a lecturer at the University of Adelaide in Australia and leads a research team in sperm and embryo biology.

After watching a space documentary with her husband, she began researching the possibility of reproduction in space.

McPherson says: "It started as just an entertaining little science-fiction idea, but it has now become an important area of research."

She and her team tested the motility of sperm under microgravity (a gravity environment similar to that of space) in a setup that simulated the reproductive tract.

They found that some sperm lost their orientation, and the number of sperm reaching the end was half that under normal gravity.

Although this study did not simulate the gravity of planets like Mars, McPherson believes it shows the importance of Earth's gravity for the normal activity of sperm.

She says: "This shows that sperm need Earth's gravity in the process of natural conception."

The problems caused by gravity do not only affect conception. Researchers have also found that microgravity affects the first 24 hours of mouse embryonic development.

McPherson says: "We saw poor results — abnormal embryonic development and some defects in early embryos."

Dr. Fathi Karouia, a senior scientist at NASA, says: "Microgravity affects cell division, organization, and communication, all of which are important for fertilization and early embryonic development."

The second major challenge of space exploration is radiation.

We know that radiation is harmful to reproductive cells, the special cells that form male sperm and female egg cells.

Earth's magnetic field protects us from most of the intense radiation in space, but beyond Earth's magnetic field — for instance on Mars — the situation is entirely different.

Dr. Abdurrahman Engin, a specialist in aerospace medicine at the University of Health Sciences in Turkey, says galactic cosmic rays (GCR) are produced by cosmic events such as supernova explosions.

These rays can penetrate the protective layers of a spacecraft and enter human tissue, with an intensity much greater than radiation on Earth.

He studied the question: in future Mars missions, if female astronauts become pregnant, would the amount of galactic cosmic-ray radiation they are exposed to exceed the level considered safe for pregnancy on Earth?

He says: "Unfortunately, the answer seems to be yes."

During the roughly six-month journey from Earth to Mars, a pregnant astronaut could be exposed to between 90 and 300 millisieverts of radiation.

That is far higher than the 5-millisievert occupational exposure limit during pregnancy recommended by the U.S. National Council on Radiation Protection and Measurements.

Such high doses could pose major risks to early-developing embryos and fetuses, and could damage their DNA.

Engin says: "During early development, cells divide rapidly and organs are being formed, so even a small disruption to DNA structure, repair, or cell growth can lead to lifelong health problems."

These risks include miscarriage, growth delays, congenital deformities, problems with the nervous system, and an increased risk of childhood cancer.

Dr. Engin says some methods — such as spacecraft shielding or timing missions to periods of low solar activity — can reduce risk, but blocking cosmic rays entirely is "very difficult".

Karouia notes that radiation and microgravity together "fundamentally affect biology", but there are other problems too.

He says: "Spaceflight also disrupts hormonal balance, sleep cycles, and circadian rhythms — all of which are closely linked to reproductive health."

He explains that when these factors act together, they can affect the quality of sperm or eggs, and the hormones needed for fetal growth during pregnancy.

Understanding whether humans can reproduce in space is extremely important for thinking about how to continue life beyond Earth.

Jan-Bernd Stukenborg, professor of molecular and cellular biology at Uppsala University in Sweden, says this concerns not only human reproduction in space but also the health and safety of astronauts.

He notes that even if humans are not yet reproducing in space, astronauts and their reproductive cells are already being exposed to the space environment. Studying the effects of radiation on reproductive cells may help us find protective methods.

Stukenborg says: "If skin cells become damaged (because of space travel), then when the person dies, that skin cell will be lost with them."

But even if astronauts are not now reproducing in space, damage to reproductive cells can be passed on to the next generation.

He says: "So I think this research is very important."

McPherson says further research in this field can help us understand more fundamental questions in human biology.

For example, her research has found that sperm which successfully reach the finish line in experiments appear to have certain features that also give them an advantage in fertilization and early development.

She says: "If we can understand more deeply why these sperm succeed, then we can make better choices in in-vitro fertilization (IVF) or animal IVF."

Experts also believe that as more women go into space, research on reproduction in space must expand and avoid focusing only on men.

The research Stukenborg refers to shows that female astronauts in long-duration missions mainly rely on contraceptive pills to suppress menstruation.

But McPherson says the effects of the space environment on the menstrual cycle still need more research.

She says: "We can't really assess this problem at present."

Karouia says there is evidence that ovarian function may be affected by radiation, and microgravity may also affect egg maturation.

He says that one of the greatest gaps in current knowledge is the pregnancy process itself.

He says: "We have almost no modern data on how mammals carry a complete pregnancy in space, particularly with regard to embryo implantation, placental development, and organ formation."

Almost everyone agrees that there is still a great deal of important work to do.

Stukenborg says: "This should not be a taboo topic… we must consider this issue right now."
