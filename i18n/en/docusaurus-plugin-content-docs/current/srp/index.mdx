# Serbian language

## Genetic and structural classification

Indo-European family, Slavic branch, South Slavic subgroup (Western group). Part of the Serbo-Croatian dialect continuum, which also includes Croatian, Bosnian, and Montenegrin — all mutually intelligible.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 9 million speakers. Serbia (official language), Bosnia and Herzegovina (Republika Srpska), Montenegro, Kosovo, with minorities in Croatia, North Macedonia, Slovenia, Romania, and Hungary.

## Dialects

Three groups by the form of "what": Shtokavian (*što*, the basis of the standard — all BCMS languages), Kajkavian (*kaj*, mainly Croatian), and Chakavian (*ča*, the Croatian coast). Shtokavian has *Ekavian* (Serbia), *Ijekavian* (Montenegro, Bosnia, parts of Croatia), and *Ikavian* (mainly Croatian) variants.

## Phonetics

Five vowels with length opposition (long/short) and pitch accent (rising/falling). Distinctive soft consonants *ć, đ, lj, nj*.

## Writing system

Two scripts used in parallel: Cyrillic (official in Serbia) and Latin (common in the media and on the internet). Phonemic spelling — "write as you speak" — established by Vuk Karadžić.

## History

Old Church Slavonic in the Serbian recension. Vuk Karadžić's language and orthographic reform (19th c.) established the standard based on Eastern Herzegovinian Shtokavian. After the breakup of Yugoslavia (1991-), the previously shared *Serbo-Croatian* norm was divided into four state standards.
