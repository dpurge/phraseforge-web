# Latin language

## Genetic and structural classification

Indo-European family, Italic branch, Latino-Faliscan subgroup. An inflected language — six cases, three genders, five declensions, four conjugations.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

A historical language — extinct as a spoken language. Today the official language of the Vatican City. Used in scientific terminology (biology, medicine, law), in the Roman Catholic liturgy, and in classical education.

## Dialects

Archaic Latin (to the 3rd c. BC), Classical (1st c. BC - 1st c. AD — Cicero, Virgil), Silver Age, Vulgar (popular — the ancestor of the Romance languages), Late, Medieval, Renaissance, Ecclesiastical, and Modern ("Neo-Latin").

## Phonetics

Five long and short vowels (a, e, i, o, u and y in loanwords). Diphthongs *ae, oe, au*. Stress on the penultimate syllable if it is long; otherwise on the antepenultimate.

## Writing system

Latin alphabet (23 classical letters; *J*, *U*, *W* were added in the Middle Ages and the modern period). Adopted from the Greek via Etruscan.

## History

Originally the dialect of Latium. With the expansion of Rome it became the language of the entire empire. After the fall of the Western Empire, Vulgar Latin evolved into the Romance languages; Classical Latin remained the language of the Church, science, and diplomacy until the 18th century.
