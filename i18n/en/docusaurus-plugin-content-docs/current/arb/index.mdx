# Arabic

Standard Arabic (`arb`) covers the modern written and formal variety used in
education, media, administration, and cross-regional communication.

## Genealogical Classification

Arabic belongs to the Afro-Asiatic family and the Semitic branch. Within the
Semitic languages, it forms the Arabic group, historically connected with the
varieties of the Arabian Peninsula and the classical literary tradition.

## Speakers and Distribution

Britannica reports that Standard Arabic is used by more than 200 million people
as a first language and by about 250 million more as a second language. In daily
life, communication often uses regional Arabic varieties, while the standard
serves as a shared written and formal language across North Africa, the Arabian
Peninsula, and much of the Middle East.

## History

The earliest forms of Arabic developed on the Arabian Peninsula. Classical
Arabic became especially important through pre-Islamic literature, the Quran,
and the expansion of Islamic culture. Modern Standard Arabic keeps strong
continuity with Classical Arabic, but it is used mainly in modern writing,
media, education, and formal situations.
