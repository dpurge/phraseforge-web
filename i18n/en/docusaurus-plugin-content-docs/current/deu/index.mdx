# German language

## Genetic and structural classification

Indo-European family, Germanic branch, West Germanic subgroup. An inflected language — four cases, three genders, strong and weak verb conjugations.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 95 million native speakers. Germany, Austria, Switzerland (in some cantons), Liechtenstein, Luxembourg, Belgium (east), South Tyrol.

## Dialects

Divided into *Hochdeutsch* (High German) and *Niederdeutsch* (Low German) along the Benrath line. Major dialects: Bavarian, Alemannic (Swiss German), Saxon, Franconian, Low German, Colonian-Central Franconian.

## Phonetics

Umlauts *ä*, *ö*, *ü*. Characteristic *ch* — a velar or palatal fricative depending on context. *Auslautverhärtung* — final-obstruent devoicing.

## Writing system

Latin alphabet with the umlauts *ä ö ü* and the letter *ß* (eszett). Nouns are capitalized. Until the 20th century, Gothic script (*Fraktur*) was also used.

## History

Old High German (8th-11th c.), Middle High German (11th-14th c.), Early New High German — codified by Luther (Bible, 1534). Spelling reform 1996/2006.
