# Tatar language

## Genetic and structural classification

Turkic family, Kipchak branch, Kipchak-Bulgar (Uralian) subgroup. Agglutinative, SOV word order, vowel harmony.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 5.2 million speakers. Tatarstan (Russian Federation — the second official language alongside Russian), Bashkortostan, other regions of Russia, Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Turkey, China.

## Dialects

Three main dialects: Central (*Kazan* — the basis of the literary standard), Western (*Mishar*), and Eastern (*Siberian Tatar*, sometimes treated as a separate language).

## Phonetics

Nine vowels with palatal and labial harmony. Short vowels are shifted (compared with other Turkic languages): short *i, e, u, ü, ı* are reduced.

## Writing system

Historically the Arabic script, then the Latin *Jaꞑalif* (1928-1939), and from 1939 Cyrillic. The 1999 Tatar Latin alphabet law was blocked by federal authorities (2002).

## History

Descended from the language of the Volga Bulgars and the languages of the Golden Horde. Classical literary language of the Volga region (*Old Tatar*, 16th-19th c.) — based on Chagatai. Modern literary Tatar at the turn of the 19th/20th century (Qayum Nasıri, Ğabdulla Tuqay).
