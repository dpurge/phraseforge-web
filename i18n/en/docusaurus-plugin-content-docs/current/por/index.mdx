# Portuguese language

## Genetic and structural classification

Indo-European family, Romance branch, Ibero-Romance subgroup. Closely related to Galician (shared origin in *galego-português*).

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 260 million speakers. Portugal, Brazil, Angola, Mozambique, Cape Verde, Guinea-Bissau, São Tomé and Príncipe, East Timor, Macau.

## Dialects

Two main standards: European (Portugal) and Brazilian — differing in phonetics, lexicon, and some grammatical constructions. African and Asian varieties. The *Acordo Ortográfico* of 1990 partially unified spelling.

## Phonetics

Nasal vowels (*ã, ẽ, ĩ, õ, ũ*) and nasal diphthongs (*ão*). Strong reduction of unstressed vowels in European pronunciation. Distinctive *r* (often uvular in Brazil).

## Writing system

Latin alphabet with diacritics: tilde (*ã, õ*), acute, grave and circumflex accents (*á, à, â, é, ê*), and cedilla (*ç*).

## History

Developed from Galician-Portuguese (12th-14th c.) in the area of Galicia and the County of Portugal. From the 15th century, colonial expansion carried it to every continent. Literary standardization with Luís de Camões (*Os Lusíadas*, 1572).
