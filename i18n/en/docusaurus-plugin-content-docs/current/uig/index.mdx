# Uyghur language

## Genetic and structural classification

Turkic family, Karluk branch, Eastern subgroup. Closely related to Uzbek. Agglutinative, SOV word order, vowel harmony (weakened).

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 10-12 million speakers. Xinjiang (Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region in China), with minorities in Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan, and Turkey.

## Dialects

Three main groups: Central (*Ürümqi*-Tarbagatay — the basis of the literary standard), Southern (Khotan, Kashgar), and Eastern (Hami, Lopnor).

## Phonetics

Eight vowels; palatal harmony is present but less strict than in Kazakh or Turkish. Characteristic weakening of unstressed vowels.

## Writing system

Perso-Arabic alphabet (the official script in the PRC — *Uyghur Ereb Yéziqi*). In parallel, it is also written in Cyrillic (the CIS countries) and in a Latin alphabet (*Uyghur Latin Yéziqi*, used e.g. in electronic communication). Earlier (1965-1982) a Chinese Zhuyin-like alphabet was tried.

## History

Descended from Old Uyghur — the language of the Uyghur Khaganate (9th-13th c.), written first in the Orkhon script and later in its own Old Uyghur alphabet (from Aramaic — the basis of the later Mongolian script). In the 15th-19th centuries, the literary language of Central Asia (Chagatai). Modern norm standardized in the 20th century — followed by successive alphabet reforms.
