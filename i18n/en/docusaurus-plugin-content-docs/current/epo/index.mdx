# Esperanto

## Genetic and structural classification

A constructed (planned) language, designed as an international auxiliary language. Vocabulary draws on Romance, Germanic and Slavic languages. Agglutinative, regular morphology.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

Hard to estimate: tens of thousands to about two million active speakers. A few thousand *denaskuloj* — people for whom Esperanto is a native language. The community is spread worldwide.

## Dialects

No real dialects. Minor idiomatic differences in usage stem from speakers' native languages. The schismatic reform Ido (1907) is now a separate language.

## Phonetics

Five vowels (a, e, i, o, u). 23 consonants. Each character is pronounced the same way every time. Stress always falls on the penultimate syllable.

## Writing system

28-letter Latin alphabet with letters with a circumflex (*ĉ ĝ ĥ ĵ ŝ*) and *ŭ* (short u). Online, *x* or *h* substitutes are commonly used.

## History

Published in 1887 in Warsaw by Ludwik Lejzer Zamenhof under the pseudonym *Doktoro Esperanto* ("one who hopes"). The first World Congress was held in Boulogne-sur-Mer in 1905. The Akademio de Esperanto safeguards the *Fundamento de Esperanto*.
