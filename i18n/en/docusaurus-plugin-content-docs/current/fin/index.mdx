# Finnish language

## Genetic and structural classification

Uralic family, Finno-Ugric branch, Finnic (Baltic-Finnic) subgroup. An agglutinative language with 15 cases, no grammatical gender, and no articles.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 5.4 million speakers. Finland, a minority in Sweden (*Tornedalsfinska*, Meänkieli — a separate language), Norway (Kven), Estonia, and Russia (Karelia).

## Dialects

Two main groups: western (including Southwestern, Tavastian, Ostrobothnian) and eastern (Savonian, Southeastern Karelian). The literary standard *yleiskieli* differs significantly from colloquial speech *puhekieli*.

## Phonetics

Eight vowels with vowel harmony (a/ä, o/ö, u/y). Vowel and consonant length distinguish meaning (*tuli* / *tuuli* / *tulli*). Stress is always on the first syllable.

## Writing system

29-letter Latin alphabet with *ä* and *ö* added. Strictly phonemic spelling.

## History

Earliest records from the 13th century. The first printed book was *Abckiria* by Mikael Agricola, 1543 (the creator of written Finnish). Standardization in the 19th century — the era of Elias Lönnrot (*Kalevala*, 1835).
