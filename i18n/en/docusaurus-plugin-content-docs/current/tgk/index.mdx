# Tajik language

## Genetic and structural classification

Indo-European family, Iranian branch, Southwestern subgroup. The Eastern variety of Persian (*fārsi*) — lexically and grammatically close to Iranian Persian and Afghan *Dari*, with strong Uzbek-Turkic and Russian influences.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 8-10 million speakers. Tajikistan (state language), Uzbekistan (Samarkand, Bukhara), Afghanistan (as part of the *Dari* continuum), Kyrgyzstan, Russia (labor migration).

## Dialects

Two groups: Northern (with Khujandi, Sogdian, Samarkand, and Bukhara dialects) and Southern (Kulobi, Badakhshani, Hisori). The Badakhshan language in Gorno-Badakhshan borders on the Pamir languages (separate Eastern Iranian languages).

## Phonetics

Six vowels. No grammatical gender, no nominal inflection. Stress on the final syllable. Vocabulary has a strong Perso-Arabic layer along with Turkic influence.

## Writing system

Cyrillic (since 1940) with the additional letters *ғ ӣ қ ӯ ҳ ҷ*. Previously the Perso-Arabic alphabet (to 1928), then Latin (1928-1940). Attempts to bring back the Persian alphabet remain symbolic.

## History

The Eastern variety of New Persian, used in Bukhara and Samarkand from the 10th-11th century. The literary language of Bukhara the Golden (Rudaki and Ferdowsi originally wrote in this tradition). After incorporation into the USSR — administratively separated from Persian, the alphabet was changed to emphasize its distinctness. Independence in 1991.
