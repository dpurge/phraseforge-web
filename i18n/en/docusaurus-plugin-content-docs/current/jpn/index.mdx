# Japanese language

## Genetic and structural classification

Japonic (Japanese-Ryukyuan) family. Its genealogical relationship to other languages is uncertain — often treated as a language isolate. Agglutinative, with SOV word order.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 125 million speakers. Japan. Smaller communities in the USA (Hawaii, California), Brazil, and Peru.

## Dialects

Two main groups: Eastern (*Tōkyō-ben* — the basis of the standard) and Western (including *Kansai-ben*, *Ōsaka-ben*, *Kyōto-ben*). Distinctly separate dialects: Tōhoku (north), Kyushu (southwest), and the Ryukyuan languages (separate languages).

## Phonetics

Five vowels (a, i, u, e, o). The mora — the rhythmic unit of the syllable. Pitch accent distinguishes meaning (e.g. *hashi*). No dynamic stress.

## Writing system

Mixed: *kanji* (Chinese characters), *hiragana* (a syllabary for native words and inflections), *katakana* (a syllabary for loanwords). Sometimes also Latin transliteration (*rōmaji*).

## History

Old Japanese (7th-8th c. — *Kojiki*, *Man'yōshū*). Classical Heian period (9th-12th c.). Middle Japanese. Reforms of the written language in the Meiji era and after 1945 (simplification of *kanji*, the *jōyō kanji* list).
