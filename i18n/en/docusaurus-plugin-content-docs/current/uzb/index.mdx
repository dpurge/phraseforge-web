# Uzbek language

## Genetic and structural classification

Turkic family, Karluk branch. Closely related to Uyghur. Agglutinative, SOV word order. Standard Uzbek has lost classical vowel harmony under Persian (Tajik) influence.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 35 million speakers. Uzbekistan (state language), Afghanistan (one of the official languages), Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Kazakhstan, Turkmenistan, Russia.

## Dialects

Three groups: Karluk (central, the basis of the standard — Tashkent, Samarkand), Kipchak (west, north), and Oghuz (Khiva, the Khorezm region, close to Turkmen). The standard preserves Karluk features with strong Persian influence.

## Phonetics

Six vowels. No vowel harmony in the literary standard (preserved in Kipchak and Oghuz dialects). Stress is usually on the final syllable.

## Writing system

Latin alphabet (since 1993, with further reforms in 1995, 2019, 2021 — still in progress). Cyrillic is still in use in the media and administration. Earlier: Perso-Arabic alphabet (until 1928), Latin *Jaꞑalif* (1928-1940), Cyrillic (1940-1993).

## History

Descended from Chagatai — the shared medieval literary language of Central Asia (Mir Ali Sher Nava'i, 15th c.). Standardized as a separate language during the Soviet period — the Tashkent dialect with weakened harmony was chosen. After independence in 1991 — successive alphabet reforms.
