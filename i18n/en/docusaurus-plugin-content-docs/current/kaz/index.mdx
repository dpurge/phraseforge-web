# Kazakh language

## Genetic and structural classification

Turkic family, Kipchak branch, Kipchak-Nogai subgroup. Agglutinative, SOV word order, vowel harmony.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 14 million speakers. Kazakhstan (the state language), China (Xinjiang), Mongolia (Bayan-Ölgii), Russia, Uzbekistan, Turkey, Iran.

## Dialects

Three groups: Western, Southern (the standard), and Northeastern. Dialectal differences are slight. Kazakhs in China and Mongolia show some distinct features.

## Phonetics

Nine vowels with palatal (front/back) and labial (rounded/unrounded) harmony. Stress is usually on the final syllable.

## Writing system

Historically written in the Perso-Arabic script (until 1929), then the Latin *Jaꞑalip* (1929-1940), and in Cyrillic (from 1940). Reform 2017-2031: a gradual transition to a Latin alphabet.

## History

Descended from the Kipchak languages of the Golden Horde. Literary standardization in the 19th century (Abai Qunanbaiuly). After 1991 — revival and status as the state language of an independent Kazakhstan.
