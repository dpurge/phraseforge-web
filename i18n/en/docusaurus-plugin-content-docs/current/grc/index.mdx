# Ancient Greek language

## Genetic and structural classification

Indo-European family, independent Hellenic branch. An inflected language — five cases, three genders, three numbers (including the dual), and a rich system of tense and aspect.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

A historical language — extinct. Used in classical scholarship and in the Eastern Orthodox liturgical tradition (in its Koine form). Its modern descendant is [Modern Greek](../ell).

## Dialects

Four main ancient dialects: Ionic-Attic (Attica, Ionian colonies), Doric (Peloponnese, Crete, Sicily), Aeolic (Lesbos, Boeotia, Thessaly), and Arcado-Cypriot. The Hellenistic *Koine* (from the 4th c. BC) — based on Attic.

## Phonetics

Long and short vowels (five pairs). Musical (pitch) accent — acute, grave, circumflex. Two aspirations: *psili* (smooth) and *dasia* (rough).

## Writing system

Greek alphabet (24 letters, from about the 8th c. BC) — an adaptation of the Phoenician script. Written with diacritics: accents, breathings, *iota subscript*. Earlier, Linear B (Mycenaean, ca. 1450-1200 BC).

## History

Mycenaean (15th-12th c. BC), Dark Ages, Archaic period (Homer, 8th c. BC), Classical (5th-4th c. BC — Plato, Thucydides), Hellenistic (*Koine* — Septuagint, New Testament), Byzantine. The basis of European scientific terminology.
