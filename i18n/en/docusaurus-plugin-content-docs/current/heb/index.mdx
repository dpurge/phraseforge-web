# Hebrew language

## Genetic and structural classification

Afro-Asiatic family, Semitic branch, Northwest (Canaanite) subgroup. Triconsonantal root system with verb conjugations (*binyanim*).

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 9 million speakers. Israel (official language) and the Jewish diaspora. The liturgical language of Judaism worldwide.

## Dialects

Modern Israeli Hebrew. Liturgical pronunciation traditions: Ashkenazi, Sephardi, Yemenite, Mizrahi. The Israeli standard is based largely on Sephardi pronunciation.

## Phonetics

Biblical Hebrew had five pairs of long and short vowels, along with pharyngeal and emphatic consonants. In Modern Israeli Hebrew — five vowels and most pharyngeals are simplified or lost.

## Writing system

Hebrew alphabet (22 consonants), written right to left. Vowels are optionally written with diacritics (*nikud*) — used in religious texts, poetry, and teaching materials.

## History

Biblical Hebrew (from about the 10th c. BC) — the Torah, Tanakh. Mishnaic Hebrew (to the 3rd c. AD). Medieval Rabbinic Hebrew. Revived as a living spoken language by Eliezer Ben-Yehuda at the end of the 19th century.
