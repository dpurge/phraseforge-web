# Italian language

## Genetic and structural classification

Indo-European family, Romance (Italic) branch, Italo-Western subgroup. The closest to Latin among the major Romance languages. Inflected in the verb.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 65 million native speakers. Italy, Switzerland (Ticino, Grisons), San Marino, Vatican City, with minorities in Croatia and Slovenia. Large diaspora (Argentina, USA, Australia).

## Dialects

Traditionally divided along the La Spezia-Rimini line: northern dialects (Gallo-Italic — Piedmontese, Lombard, Venetian, Ligurian; and Lower Italian) and central-southern dialects (Tuscan, Central Italian, Neapolitan, Sicilian). The literary standard is based on the Tuscan dialect.

## Phonetics

Seven vowels (open and close *e*, *o*). Consonant gemination distinguishes meaning (*pala* / *palla*). Stress is most often on the penultimate syllable.

## Writing system

21-letter Latin alphabet (the letters *j, k, w, x, y* appear only in loanwords). Acute and grave accents on final vowels.

## History

Descended from Vulgar Latin, through *toscano fiorentino*. The earliest texts (9th-10th c.) — *Indovinello veronese*, *Placiti cassinesi*. The "three crowns" of the 14th century (Dante, Petrarch, Boccaccio) established the literary model. National uniformity followed unification in 1861.
