# Mongolian language

## Genetic and structural classification

Mongolic family (part of the hypothetical Altaic group). Agglutinative, SOV word order, vowel harmony.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 6 million speakers. Mongolia (Khalkha), China — Inner Mongolia (Chakhar, Ordos), Russia (Buryat, Kalmyk — often treated as separate languages).

## Dialects

Two standards: Khalkha (*Chalch* — Mongolia, in Cyrillic) and Chakhar (Inner Mongolia, in the traditional script). Other varieties: Ordos, Oirat, Buryat.

## Phonetics

Seven vowels with harmony (front/back, pharyngeal). Long and short vowels. Stress on the first syllable.

## Writing system

The traditional *Mongolian script* (derived from Uyghur-Aramaic, used since the 13th century — written vertically) — used in China. Cyrillic (from 1946) — in Mongolia. Mongolia plans to reinstate the traditional script for official documents by 2025.

## History

Crystallized as a literary language under the empire of Genghis Khan (13th c.) — *The Secret History of the Mongols*. After the fall of the Yuan, gradual divergence of dialects. Classical Mongolian (17th-19th c.). 20th-century alphabet reforms — Cyrillic in the Mongolian People's Republic, further reforms after 1990.
