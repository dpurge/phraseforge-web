# Persian language

## Genetic and structural classification

Indo-European family, Iranian branch, Southwestern subgroup. Isolating in nominal morphology (no declension, no grammatical gender), inflected in the verb.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 80 million native speakers, more than 100 million in total. Iran (Persian / *fārsi*), Afghanistan (*Dari*), Tajikistan (Tajik, see [Tajik](../tgk)).

## Dialects

Three main standard varieties: Iranian *fārsi*, Afghan *Dari*, and Tajik. Internal dialects: Shirazi, Isfahani, Mazandarani, Gilaki (some treated as separate languages).

## Phonetics

Six vowels. No grammatical gender, no cases. The historical short/long vowel distinction has been reduced to a qualitative difference. Stress falls on the final syllable.

## Writing system

Perso-Arabic alphabet (based on Arabic, 32 letters — the letters *پ چ ژ گ* are added). Written right to left. Tajik uses Cyrillic.

## History

Old Persian (Achaemenid era, 6th-4th c. BC, cuneiform script), Middle Persian / Pahlavi (Sasanian era), New Persian from the 9th century AD — *fārsi-ye dari*. Literary classics: Ferdowsi, Hafez, Rumi.
