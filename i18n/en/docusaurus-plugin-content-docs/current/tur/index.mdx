# Turkish language

## Genetic and structural classification

Turkic family, Oghuz branch, Western Oghuz subgroup. Agglutinative, SOV word order, vowel harmony, no grammatical gender, no articles.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 80 million speakers. Turkey, Northern Cyprus, with minorities in Bulgaria, Greece, North Macedonia, Germany (a strong diaspora), the Netherlands, and Austria.

## Dialects

The literary standard is based on the Istanbul dialect. Anatolian dialects (dozens of varieties) and Rumelian dialects (the Balkans). Related languages: Azerbaijani, Gagauz, Turkmen, Crimean Tatar.

## Phonetics

Eight vowels with palatal (front/back) and labial (rounded/unrounded) harmony. Distinctive *ı* (a back, unrounded *i*) and *ğ* (a letter that lengthens the preceding vowel).

## Writing system

Latin alphabet (29 letters, without *q, w, x*; with *ç, ğ, ı, İ, ö, ş, ü*) introduced in 1928 as part of Atatürk's reforms. Earlier the Ottoman Turkish alphabet (Perso-Arabic).

## History

Descended from the Oghuz dialects of the Seljuk tribes (11th c.). Ottoman Turkish (14th-20th c.) — heavily saturated with Arabic and Persian, used in writing. The language reform of the 1930s (*Türk Dil Kurumu*) replaced Perso-Arabic loanwords with native Turkic neologisms.
