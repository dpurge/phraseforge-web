# Swedish language

## Genetic and structural classification

Indo-European family, Germanic branch, North Germanic subgroup (East Scandinavian). Mutually intelligible with Norwegian and Danish.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 10 million speakers. Sweden, Finland (the second official language, around 290,000 — *Finland Swedish*), Estonia (a historical minority), and the diaspora in the USA and Canada.

## Dialects

Six traditional groups: Norrlandic (north), Svealandic (central, including Stockholm — the basis of the standard), Götaland (west), Gotlandic (Gotland), Scanian (south, close to Danish), and Finland Swedish. Distinctive varieties include Älvdalian (*övdalsk*, sometimes treated as a separate language).

## Phonetics

Nine vowels with length opposition. Characteristic *sj*-/*tj*-laut (palato-velar fricatives). Pitch accent (two tones — *grav* and *akut*) distinguishes word pairs.

## Writing system

Latin alphabet with the letters *å, ä, ö*. Spelling based on the reforms of 1906 (Adolf Noreen).

## History

From Old Norse (to about 1225). Classical Old Swedish period (1225-1526) — *Magnus Erikssons landslag*. Modern Swedish — the Gustav Vasa Bible (1541), Lutheran Reformation. The 1906 reform modernized spelling.
