# Romanian language

## Genetic and structural classification

Indo-European family, Romance branch, Eastern Romance subgroup. The only Romance language in an environment surrounded by Slavic, Finno-Ugric, and Turkic languages.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 24 million speakers. Romania, Moldova (where it is sometimes called *limba moldovenească*), and minorities in Ukraine, Serbia, and Hungary.

## Dialects

Four historical Eastern Romance varieties (sometimes treated as separate languages): *Daco-Romanian* (Standard Romanian), *Aromanian* (the Balkans), *Megleno-Romanian* (North Macedonia, Greece), *Istro-Romanian* (Croatia, endangered). Daco-Romanian dialects: Muntenian (the basis of the standard), Moldavian, Banatian, Crișana, Maramureș.

## Phonetics

Seven vowels (a, e, i, o, u, ă, î/â). Diphthongs *ea, oa*. Mobile stress. Palatalization of final *i* (silent or strong).

## Writing system

Latin alphabet with diacritics: *ă, â, î, ș, ț*. Until 1860 the Cyrillic script was used (a Romanian Cyrillic based on Slavic). Moldova used Cyrillic during the Soviet era (until 1989).

## History

Descended from the Latin of the province of Dacia (2nd c. AD). Isolation from the rest of Romania resulted in significant Slavic influence on vocabulary and structure. The earliest surviving letter in Romanian — *Scrisoarea lui Neacșu*, 1521. The switch from Cyrillic to the Latin alphabet in the 19th century symbolized Westernization.
