# Indonesian language

## Genetic and structural classification

Austronesian family, Malayo-Polynesian branch. A standardized variety of Malay. Agglutinative morphology, no grammatical gender, no nominal inflection.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 45 million native speakers, more than 200 million in total (as a second language). Indonesia — the national language in a country of more than 700 local languages.

## Dialects

The written standard (*bahasa baku*) is based on the Riau-Johor variety of Malay. The colloquial register (*bahasa gaul*) is strongly influenced by Jakartan slang and regional varieties. Closely related to Malaysian (*Bahasa Melayu*).

## Phonetics

Six vowels (a, e, i, o, u, ə). Simple consonant system without aspiration or palatalization. Weak stress, usually on the penultimate syllable.

## Writing system

Latin alphabet. Spelling was reformed in 1972 — *Ejaan Yang Disempurnakan* (EYD) — and aligned with Malaysian.

## History

Old Malay (from the 7th century — Srivijaya inscriptions). Classical Malay during the sultanates (15th-19th c.). At the Youth Congress of 1928, Malay was adopted under the name *Bahasa Indonesia* as the national language. After independence (1945) — the official language.
