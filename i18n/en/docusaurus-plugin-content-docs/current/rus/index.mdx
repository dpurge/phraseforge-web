# Russian language

## Genetic and structural classification

Indo-European family, Slavic branch, East Slavic subgroup (together with Ukrainian and Belarusian). An inflected language with six cases, three genders, and a perfective/imperfective aspect distinction.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 150 million native speakers, more than 250 million in total. Russia, Belarus, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Moldova, the Baltic states, and the former Soviet republics.

## Dialects

Three dialect groups: Northern (*okanye* — full pronunciation of unstressed *o*), Southern (*akanye* — reduction of *o* to *a*), and Central (mixed features, the basis of the standard — Moscow). Dialectal variation is relatively small.

## Phonetics

Vowels are reduced in unstressed positions. Hard and soft (palatalized) consonants are in phonemic opposition. Mobile, free stress.

## Writing system

Cyrillic — 33 letters. The 1918 reform removed the letters *і, ѣ, ѳ, ѵ* and the hard sign *ъ* in word-final position.

## History

Old Russian (11th-15th c.) — the common East Slavic ancestor. Old Church Slavonic as the liturgical and literary language. Peter the Great's reforms (18th c. — *grazhdanka*, the civil alphabet). Literary standardization in the 19th century (Pushkin). The 1918 reform.
