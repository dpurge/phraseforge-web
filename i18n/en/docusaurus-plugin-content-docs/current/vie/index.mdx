# Vietnamese language

## Genetic and structural classification

Austroasiatic family, Mon-Khmer branch, Vietic (Viet-Muong) subgroup. Tonal, isolating, monosyllabic in root morphology, with SVO word order.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 85 million speakers. Vietnam (official language), with diaspora communities in the USA, Australia, France, Germany, the Czech Republic, and Poland.

## Dialects

Three main varieties: Northern (Hanoi — the literary standard), Central (Huế), and Southern (Saigon). They differ mainly in the realization of tones and some consonants.

## Phonetics

Six tones in the Northern variety (*ngang, huyền, sắc, hỏi, ngã, nặng*); five in the Southern. A rich vowel system (11 monophthongs, plus diphthongs). Final consonants are limited.

## Writing system

*Chữ Quốc Ngữ* — Latin alphabet with rich diacritics (tone marks plus vowel marks). Developed by Catholic missionaries in the 17th century (Alexandre de Rhodes); official since 1910. Earlier: Chinese characters (*chữ Hán*) and locally developed *chữ Nôm* (Chinese-derived characters).

## History

Separated from the Viet-Muong group in the first millennium AD. Heavily influenced by Chinese — about 60% of the vocabulary consists of Chinese loanwords. Independence from China (10th c.) → development of *chữ Nôm*. The colonial introduction of the Latin alphabet in the 19th-20th centuries replaced the Chinese-derived scripts.
