# Swahili language

## Genetic and structural classification

Niger-Congo family, Atlantic-Congo branch, Bantu subgroup (code G.42 in Guthrie's classification). Agglutinative, with a system of more than a dozen noun classes marked by prefixed concord.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 15-20 million native speakers, more than 200 million in total. A lingua franca of East and Central Africa: Tanzania, Kenya, Uganda, DR Congo, Rwanda, Burundi, parts of Mozambique, Somalia, and Comoros.

## Dialects

The standard *Kiunguja* (based on Zanzibari) — the basis of *Kiswahili Sanifu*. Other coastal dialects: *Kimvita* (Mombasa), *Kiamu* (Lamu), *Kipemba* (Pemba), and Comorian (sometimes a separate language).

## Phonetics

A simple five-vowel system (a, e, i, o, u) without phonemic length. No tones. Regular stress on the penultimate syllable. Prenasalized consonants (*mb, nd, ng, nj*).

## Writing system

Today written in the Latin alphabet. Historically *Ajami* — a variant of the Arabic script with additional letters for sounds with no Arabic counterpart. The oldest Ajami manuscripts go back to the 17th century.

## History

Originated among the Bantu population of the East African coast, in long contact with Arab and Persian traders (Arabic loanwords are estimated at 15-30% of the vocabulary). Standardized in the colonial period (1930s — the *East African Swahili Committee*). After independence — a national language of Tanzania and Kenya.
