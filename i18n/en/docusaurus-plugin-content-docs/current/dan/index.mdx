# Danish language

## Genetic and structural classification

Indo-European family, Germanic branch, North Germanic subgroup (East Scandinavian). Closely related to Swedish and Norwegian.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 6 million speakers. Denmark, the Faroe Islands, Greenland (as a second language), and a minority in Schleswig-Holstein (Germany).

## Dialects

Three main groups: Jutlandic (west), Insular (Zealand, Funen), and East Danish (Bornholm). The standard *rigsdansk* is based on the Copenhagen dialect.

## Phonetics

Distinctive *stød* — a glottal constriction / laryngealization that distinguishes word pairs. Very strong vowel reduction in colloquial speech; spelling diverges from pronunciation.

## Writing system

Latin alphabet with the letters *æ*, *ø*, *å* (the latter was written as *aa* until 1948).

## History

Descended from Old Norse — the common ancestor of the Scandinavian languages. Early Danish (1100-1525), Middle Danish, Modern Danish. The Reformation in the 16th century established the written norm (Christian III Bible, 1550).
