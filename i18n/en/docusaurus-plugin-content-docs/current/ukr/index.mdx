# Ukrainian language

## Genetic and structural classification

Indo-European family, Slavic branch, East Slavic subgroup (together with Russian and Belarusian). An inflected language with seven cases (including the vocative) and a perfective/imperfective aspect distinction.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 33 million speakers. Ukraine (official language), with large minorities in Russia, Canada, Poland, the USA, Moldova, and Belarus.

## Dialects

Three groups: Northern (Polissian), Southwestern (Galician, Lemko, Bukovinian, Podolian, Dniester), and Southeastern (Dnieper, Slobozhanshchyna — the basis of the literary standard). *Surzhyk* — a Ukrainian-Russian mixture, not recognized as a standard.

## Phonetics

Six vowels with no reduction in unstressed positions. The vowel *и* [ɪ]. Distinctive *г* — a voiced glottal fricative [ɦ], differing from Russian [g]. Mobile stress.

## Writing system

Cyrillic — 33 letters, including the distinctive *і*, *ї*, *є*, *ґ*. The letter *ы* is absent (replaced by *и*).

## History

Separated from the East Slavic community in the 14th-15th centuries. *Old Ruthenian* in the Grand Duchy of Lithuania. Modern codification in the 19th century — Ivan Kotliarevsky (*Eneida*, 1798), Taras Shevchenko. The 1928 spelling reform (Kharkiv, partially reversed by the Soviets) and the new reform of 2019.
