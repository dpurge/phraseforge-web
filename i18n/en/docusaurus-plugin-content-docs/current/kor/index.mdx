# Korean language

## Genetic and structural classification

Koreanic (Korean-Jeju) family. Proposed kinship with other families (Altaic, Japonic) is unconfirmed — most often treated as a language isolate. Agglutinative, SOV word order. Extensive honorific system.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 80 million speakers. South Korea, North Korea, China (Jilin Province), Japan, and the diaspora (USA, Russia, Uzbekistan — *Koryo-saram*).

## Dialects

The South Korean standard *Pyojuneo* (based on Seoul) and the North Korean *Munhwaeo* (based on Pyongyang). Main dialects: Gyeongsang (southeast), Jeolla (southwest), Hamgyong, Pyongan, Jeju (sometimes considered a separate language).

## Phonetics

Three-way distinction of stop consonants: plain, tense (*ssanggireu*), and aspirated. 10 simple vowels (formally). Dynamic stress is weak; some dialects have length distinction or pitch accent.

## Writing system

*Hangul* (한글) — a featural alphabet created in 1443 by King Sejong the Great, promulgated in *Hunminjeongeum*. Letters are arranged into syllabic blocks. *Hanja* (Chinese characters) appear occasionally in academic and press writing.

## History

Old Korean (7th-10th c.) — written with borrowed Chinese characters (*Idu*, *Hyangchal*). Middle Korean (10th-16th c.). After the introduction of Hangul — coexistence with Hanja. After 1945 — independent evolution in North and South Korea.
