# Dutch language

## Genetic and structural classification

Indo-European family, Germanic branch, West Germanic subgroup (Low Franconian). Closely related to Low German, Frisian, and English. Inflected in the verb, reduced in nominal morphology.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 24 million native speakers. The Netherlands, Belgium (Flanders — *Vlaams*), Suriname, the Dutch Caribbean. Afrikaans in South Africa and Namibia — a descendant of Dutch, now a separate language.

## Dialects

Three groups: Hollandic (west, the basis of the standard), Brabantian-Limburgish (south), and Low Saxon (northeast). Flemish (Belgium) is close to the standard but has its own accent and vocabulary.

## Phonetics

A rich vowel system, including long and short vowels and diphthongs (*ij, ui, ou*). Characteristic *g* / *ch* — velar/palatal fricatives. *R* is realized variously (apical, uvular).

## Writing system

Latin alphabet. The characteristic digraph *ij* is sometimes treated as a single letter. Spelling reform of 1947 and later updates (1996, 2005).

## History

Old Dutch (to about 1150), Middle Dutch (1150-1500), Modern Dutch — with the Statenvertaling Bible (1637) as a key step toward standardization. The Golden Age (17th c.) established the position of the literary language.
