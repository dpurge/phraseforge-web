# Spanish language

## Genetic and structural classification

Indo-European family, Romance branch, Ibero-Romance subgroup. Also called *Castilian* (*castellano*). Inflected in the verb.

## Number of users and geographic distribution

About 480 million native speakers, more than 590 million in total. Spain, Latin America (except Brazil), Equatorial Guinea, minorities in the USA, the Philippines (residual), and Western Sahara.

## Dialects

Two large groups: Castilian (Iberian Peninsula) and American. Main Latin American varieties: Mexican, Caribbean, Andean, Rioplatense (Argentina, Uruguay), Chilean. Distinguishing features: *seseo* (*c/z* pronounced as *s*), *yeísmo* (*ll* = *y*), *voseo* (*vos* instead of *tú*).

## Phonetics

Five simple vowels — no length distinction. Diphthongs *ie, ue*. Distinctive *jota* (guttural *j*) and *rr* (trill). Dynamic, mobile stress, marked orthographically when it deviates from the regular pattern.

## Writing system

27-letter Latin alphabet including *ñ*. Acute accents (*á é í ó ú*), diaeresis (*ü*). Inverted exclamation and question marks at the start of sentences (*¡! ¿?*).

## History

Descended from Vulgar Latin of the Iberian Peninsula. Old Castilian (11th-15th c.) — *Cantar de mio Cid* (ca. 1140). The Reconquista carried it south; colonial expansion in the 15th-16th centuries spread it to the Americas and the Philippines. Standardization: Real Academia Española (1713).
